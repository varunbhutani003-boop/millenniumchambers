DPDP Act Compliance for Startups in India

By Varun Bhutani, Advocate · Millennium Chambers, Gurugram · Updated 12 November 2026

The DPDP Act, 2023: what Indian startups need to know

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 set out how businesses in India may collect and use digital personal data. Most startups that run an app, a website or a customer database will be "Data Fiduciaries" under the Act. This note summarises the key obligations in plain language. It is general information only, not legal advice.

DPDP compliance timeline

  • 13 November 2025: the DPDP Rules were notified and the provisions setting up the Data Protection Board of India took effect.

  • 13 November 2026: the provisions on registration and obligations of Consent Managers take effect.

  • 13 May 2027: most core obligations of Data Fiduciaries apply, including notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, erasure and the rights of users.

Key obligations for a startup

  • Notice and consent: consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action, and preceded by a notice in clear and plain language that itemises the personal data and the purpose. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it.

  • Purpose limitation: use personal data only for the purpose the user consented to, or for a "legitimate use" recognised by the Act.

  • Security safeguards: take reasonable security measures such as encryption, access controls and monitoring; the Rules require logs to be kept for at least one year.

  • Breach intimation: inform each affected user and the Data Protection Board without delay, and send the Board a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.

  • Children's data: obtain verifiable consent of a parent before processing the personal data of anyone under 18, and do not track, behaviourally monitor or target advertising at children.

  • Grievance redressal: publish the contact details of a person who can answer questions about your data processing, and respond to grievances within the period set by the Rules (not more than 90 days).

  • Erasure: delete personal data once the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless the law requires you to keep it.

  • Vendors: engage data processors (cloud, analytics, payments, CRM) only under a valid contract; your startup remains responsible for their processing.

Rights of users (Data Principals)

Users have the right to information about how their data is processed, the right to correction and erasure, the right to grievance redressal and the right to nominate another person to exercise their rights.

Penalties under the DPDP Act

The Schedule to the Act provides for penalties of up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach, and up to ₹200 crore each for failing to notify a breach and for breaching the additional obligations on children's data. Penalties are imposed by the Data Protection Board after an inquiry.

A founder's DPDP checklist

  • Map the personal data you collect, why you collect it and where it is stored.

  • Rewrite privacy notices and consent screens in plain language (English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution).

  • Review contracts with every vendor that handles user data.

  • Prepare a breach-response plan and assign who reports to the Board.

  • Set up a grievance contact and a process for access, correction and erasure requests.
