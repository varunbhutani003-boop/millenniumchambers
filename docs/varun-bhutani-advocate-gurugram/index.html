Varun Bhutani — Advocate, Gurugram

Advocate, Corporate Lawyer and Startup Legal Advisor in Gurugram (Gurgaon)

Varun Bhutani is an advocate enrolled with the Bar Council of Delhi (since December 2018) and the Managing Advocate of Millennium Chambers, with his office at 325H, Sector 38, Gurugram (Gurgaon), Haryana. He began in litigation before the Delhi High Court and spent nearly seven years since 2019 as an in-house lawyer with India's high-growth technology companies, most recently as Head of Legal at YesMadam.

Professional background

  • Head of Legal, YesMadam Technologies (2026)

  • Head of Legal, Zypp Electric (2025–2026) — drafted the organisation's Road Safety SOP manual for its EV fleet

  • Chief Manager (Legal), Ecom Express (2024–2025)

  • Legal team, OfBusiness / Oxyzo Financial Services (2019–2024) — a founding member of the NBFC's legal team, from incorporation to systemically important NBFC and unicorn

  • Associate Advocate, Pavan Duggal Associates, Delhi High Court (2018–2019)

Areas of work

Startup and venture-capital transactions, founders' and shareholders' agreements, ESOPs, commercial contracts, fintech and NBFC regulation, debt recovery (SARFAESI, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, arbitration, Section 138 cheque-bounce cases and civil recovery suits), debt fundraising through non-convertible debentures, term loans and lease financing, IPO and DRHP readiness, the DPDP Act 2023, the four Labour Codes and gig-worker frameworks, consumer protection, road-safety SOPs and accident, insurance and MACT claims for fleets, corporate governance, ESG and EPR compliance, trademarks, commercial real estate and RERA matters before the Haryana Real Estate Regulatory Authority (HRERA).

Focus: data protection for startups

Varun advises startups on India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025, including privacy notices, consent flows, vendor contracts and breach-response planning ahead of the core obligations that apply from 13 May 2027. Read his guide: DPDP Act compliance for startups in India.

Focus: fundraising and investment documents

Varun works with founders on the documents behind a funding round: term sheets, share subscription and shareholders' agreements, founders' agreements and ESOP schemes, together with the Companies Act and FEMA steps that follow. Read his guide: term sheets for startups in India.

Focus: commercial contracts and payment recovery

Much of Varun's in-house work has been on the commercial side of fast-growing companies: vendor and customer agreements, franchise and dealer networks, payment-gateway and logistics contracts, and the notices and recoveries that follow when contracts break down. At OfBusiness and Oxyzo he worked on lending and recovery documentation from the NBFC's incorporation onwards, including matters under the IBC and SARFAESI. At Zypp Electric and Ecom Express he handled EV-fleet, delivery-partner and two-wheeler dealership arrangements, along with accident and on-ground escalations. His recovery work there ran across Section 138 cheque-bounce complaints, arbitration claims, civil suits and Economic Offences Wing complaints, up to the Supreme Court, and included the litigation for a company acquired through NCLT liquidation. See debt recovery lawyer in Gurgaon. For founders, this covers drafting contracts that are easier to enforce, replying to legal notices, and using the MSMED Act's 45-day payment rule and the MSE Facilitation Council when a micro or small supplier is not paid. Read his guide: MSME payment recovery under the MSMED Act.

Focus: legal notices, replies and pre-litigation steps

A large share of commercial disputes in Gurugram begin and often end with an exchange of legal notices. Over his in-house years with a lender, a logistics company, an EV-fleet operator and a consumer platform, Varun has worked on payment and recovery demands, Section 138 cheque-dishonour notices and responses to consumer complaints. His approach starts from the documents (the contract, invoices, ledgers and correspondence) and checks the legal timelines that a notice can trigger, such as the 15-day payment window under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, pre-institution mediation under Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015 for commercial suits, and the effect of a written acknowledgment of debt under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963. Read his guide: how to reply to a legal notice in Gurugram.

Focus: employment contracts and restrictive covenants

Having led legal teams at companies with large on-roll and gig workforces, including an EV-fleet operator, a logistics network and a beauty-services platform, Varun has drafted and reviewed appointment letters, consultant and freelancer agreements, confidentiality and intellectual-property assignment clauses, and exit documentation for employees at every level. A recurring question from founders is how far a non-compete clause can protect the business. Under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a restraint that operates after employment ends is generally void, while exclusivity during employment, garden leave, confidentiality and IP assignment can be enforced. His work in this area focuses on drafting the protections a court is likely to uphold, and on the founder and shareholder covenants that investors ask for in funding rounds. Read his guide: non-compete clauses in India under Section 27.
