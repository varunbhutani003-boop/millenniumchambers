Startup Lawyer in Gurgaon (Gurugram)

A startup lawyer in Gurgaon who has worked inside startups

Millennium Chambers is the practice of Advocate Varun Bhutani at 325H, Sector 38, Gurugram (Gurgaon), Haryana. Before starting the practice he spent about seven years as an in-house lawyer at venture-backed companies, including as Head of Legal at YesMadam and Zypp Electric and as part of the founding legal team of Oxyzo, the NBFC arm of OfBusiness. The work here is the work a founder would otherwise hand to an in-house counsel: contracts, fundraising documents, compliance and disputes, handled by someone who has sat on the company's side of the table.

What founders in Gurgaon usually come for

  • Setting up correctly. Choosing between a private limited company and an LLP, founders' agreements with vesting, IP assignment from founders and early employees, and DPIIT Startup India recognition.

  • Raising money. Term sheets, share subscription and shareholders' agreements, due diligence preparation, ESOP schemes, and the Companies Act and FEMA filings that follow a round.

  • Contracts that carry the business. Customer and vendor agreements, SaaS and master services agreements, payment-gateway and lending-partner contracts, franchise and distribution agreements, NDAs.

  • Compliance that investors and enterprise customers check. The DPDP Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025, the four Labour Codes, gig and platform-worker arrangements, POSH, consumer protection and e-commerce rules, and FSSAI labelling and claims for food and nutraceutical brands.

  • Protecting the brand. Trademark searches, filing, examination replies and oppositions.

  • Getting paid and handling disputes. Legal notices, MSME delayed-payment claims before the Facilitation Council, consumer complaints, arbitration and recovery matters.

How the work is arranged

Most startups do not need a full-time lawyer, and many cannot justify one before Series A. Work is taken either matter by matter, for example a funding round, a contract set or a single dispute, or on a monthly retainer where the practice acts as the company's outside legal team and handles whatever comes up. The first conversation is about what the business actually needs in the next six months, so that money is not spent on documents it does not need yet. Questions founders ask

When should a startup first speak to a lawyer? Before the co-founders split equity and before the first outside money or the first large customer contract. Fixing a founders' agreement, IP ownership or a cap table after a dispute or during due diligence costs far more than getting it right at the start.

Should a startup hire an in-house lawyer or use an outside lawyer? Up to roughly Series A, most startups are better served by an outside lawyer on a retainer or per-matter basis. A full-time hire makes sense when contract volume, regulatory work or litigation becomes a daily load.

Does the practice work with startups outside Gurgaon? Yes. Advisory, contract and compliance work is done remotely for companies across India. Appearances before courts and tribunals are taken in Gurugram and Delhi NCR.

Which regulated sectors does the practice have experience in? Fintech and NBFC lending, logistics and electric mobility, home-services and gig platforms, and consumer brands including food and nutraceuticals, drawn from in-house roles at Oxyzo, Ecom Express, Zypp Electric and YesMadam.
