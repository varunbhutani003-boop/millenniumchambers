Trademark Registration for Startups in India

By Varun Bhutani, Advocate · Millennium Chambers, Gurugram · Updated 12 November 2026

Why a trademark matters for an Indian startup

A startup's name, logo and tagline are often its most valuable early assets. Registering them as trademarks under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 gives the owner the exclusive right to use the mark for the registered goods or services, a clear basis to stop copycats, and an asset that investors check during due diligence.

How trademark registration works in India

1. Search before you file

Before adopting a brand, search the Trade Marks Registry's public search for identical or similar marks in the same class. A clash found early is far cheaper to fix than a rebrand after launch.

2. Choose the right class

India follows the international Nice Classification of 45 classes (34 for goods, 11 for services). A separate application or class entry is needed for each class the business actually uses or intends to use.

3. File Form TM-A

The application is filed on Form TM-A, online through the IP India portal or physically. The official fee for e-filing is lower for individuals, DPIIT-recognised startups and small enterprises (₹4,500 per class) than for other applicants (₹9,000 per class).

4. Examination and reply

The Registry examines the application and may raise objections, for example that the mark is descriptive or similar to an earlier mark. Objections must be answered within the time allowed, with a written reply and, if needed, a hearing.

5. Publication and opposition

Once accepted, the mark is advertised in the Trade Marks Journal. Any person may file an opposition within four months of the advertisement.

6. Registration and renewal

If there is no opposition, or it is decided in the applicant's favour, the mark is registered. Registration lasts ten years and can be renewed every ten years.

Practical checklist for founders

  • File in the name of the company that owns the business, not a founder personally, unless there is a deliberate reason.

  • Get written IP assignments from designers and agencies who created the logo.

  • Use ™ while the application is pending; use ® only after registration, since falsely representing a mark as registered is an offence.

  • Keep proof of first use (invoices, website archives, social posts) in case of a dispute.

  • Set reminders for replies, hearings and renewal dates.
