Licences a Gurugram Startup Needs in Its First 90 Days

By Varun Bhutani, Advocate · Millennium Chambers, Gurugram · Updated 19 November 2026

Why the order matters

A new business in Gurugram can collect a dozen registrations in its first quarter, or it can take the four or five it actually needs and add the rest when the business triggers them. The second approach is cheaper and leaves fewer returns to file. This guide sets out a practical order for a typical startup with an office in Gurugram, a small team and customers across India.

Days 1 to 30: the entity and the basics

1. Settle the structure

Most registrations attach to the entity that holds them. A founder who takes GST, a trademark and an FSSAI registration as a proprietor and incorporates a company three months later has to transfer or redo each one. If outside investment or co-founders are likely, incorporating a private limited company or LLP first saves that work. PAN and TAN are allotted with the incorporation.

2. Open the bank account and take Udyam registration

Udyam registration is free, online and based on Aadhaar and PAN. It takes a few minutes and brings the business within the delayed-payment protection of the MSMED Act, which matters as soon as the first large customer pays late. See MSME payment recovery.

3. Register the premises under the shops and establishments law

An office or shop in Gurugram is registered with the Haryana Labour Department online. The certificate also serves as address proof for the bank and for other applications.

4. File the trademark

The brand name can be searched and filed as soon as it is chosen. Filing early fixes the date of the claim, and recognised startups and small enterprises pay the lower official fee. See the trademark guide.

Days 30 to 60: tax and recognition

5. Take GST registration when it is triggered

GST registration is compulsory above the turnover threshold, and from the first sale for a business that supplies goods to another state or sells goods through an online marketplace. A software or services startup billing customers in other states is often below the threshold at first, but many register early because business customers ask for a GST invoice. Once registered, returns are due even in a month with no sales.

6. Apply for Startup India recognition

A company, LLP or registered partnership that meets the DPIIT conditions can apply for recognition online, free of charge. The application needs a short write-up on what is innovative or scalable about the business. Recognition is the gateway to the lower intellectual property fees, self-certification under some labour laws and the application for the income-tax holiday.

7. Get digital signatures in place

Directors and authorised signatories need a digital signature certificate for company, GST and trademark filings. It is a small item that holds up larger ones when it is missing.

Days 60 to 90: what the business model triggers

8. Sector registrations

  • Food and beverages: an FSSAI registration or licence before the first sale. See the 2026 FSSAI limits.

  • Imports and exports of goods: an Importer-Exporter Code from the DGFT.

  • Packaged products: Legal Metrology registration and compliant labels.

  • Specified trades and premises: a trade licence from the municipal corporation.

  • Lending, payments, insurance or investment products: check the Reserve Bank, SEBI or IRDAI position before launch, since operating without the required authorisation is a far more serious matter than a late registration.

9. Employer registrations

Provident fund and ESI registration become compulsory once headcount crosses the statutory limits, and appointment letters and wage records are required from the first hire. Haryana does not levy professional tax. See the Labour Codes guide.

10. Website and data paperwork

Terms of use, a privacy notice that matches what the product actually collects, and a grievance contact are not licences, but they are the documents customers, marketplaces and investors look at first. See the DPDP guide.

Common mistakes

  • Taking registrations in a founder's personal name and never moving them to the company.

  • Registering for GST without planning for the monthly filings, and collecting late fees on nil returns.

  • Choosing the wrong activity or category in an FSSAI or Udyam application, which surfaces later during a customer or investor check.

  • Launching a regulated product on the strength of a partner's licence without a written arrangement that the regulator would accept.

  • Letting a registration lapse after shifting office, because the address was never amended.

A short checklist

  • Entity incorporated, PAN, TAN and bank account in place.

  • Udyam and shops and establishments registrations taken.

  • Trademark filed in the company's name.

  • GST position checked and registration taken if triggered.

  • DPIIT recognition applied for.

  • Sector licences identified in writing before launch.

  • A compliance calendar for returns and renewals.
