Business Registrations and Licences in India

The registrations a small business or startup usually needs, and how each one works

Most small businesses in India need fewer registrations than their founders expect, and the common ones are applied for online at little or no government fee. The harder part is knowing which ones the business actually triggers, applying in the right name and category, and keeping up with the filings that follow. Millennium Chambers, the practice of Advocate Varun Bhutani at 325H, Sector 38, Gurugram (Gurgaon), advises founders, growing companies and businesses entering India on these registrations as part of its corporate and regulatory work.

This page describes each registration in general terms. Fees and thresholds are set by the government and change from time to time, so the official portal should be checked before applying.

Registrations most businesses take first

  • Udyam (MSME) registration. A free, paperless self-declaration on the Udyam portal, based on the Aadhaar of the proprietor, partner or director and the PAN of the business. It classifies the enterprise as micro, small or medium by investment and turnover. It is optional, but it opens the delayed-payment protection of the MSMED Act and several lending and procurement benefits. See MSME payment recovery.

  • GST registration. No government fee. It is required once turnover crosses the threshold for the state and the type of supply (in most states Rs 40 lakh for goods and Rs 20 lakh for services), and from the first sale in some cases whatever the turnover, such as supplying goods to another state or selling goods through an online marketplace. Registration is the easy part: returns then fall due every month or quarter.

  • Shops and establishments registration. A state registration for offices, shops and other commercial premises, applied for online through the state labour department. In Haryana and Delhi the certificate is usually issued quickly and is often the first address proof a bank asks for.

  • Startup India (DPIIT) recognition. A free online application for companies, LLPs and registered partnerships that meet the age and turnover limits and are working on innovation or a scalable model. Recognition allows self-certification under some labour laws, lower trademark and patent fees, and eligibility to apply for the income-tax holiday.

Registrations that depend on what the business does

  • FSSAI registration or licence. Every food business needs one, from a home kitchen to a manufacturer. Since 1 April 2026 a business with annual turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore takes the basic registration, at a government fee of Rs 100 a year. See the 2026 FSSAI changes.

  • Importer-Exporter Code (IEC). A one-time online application to the DGFT, needed before importing or exporting goods. The details are confirmed on the portal once a year.

  • Trademark. Filing is simple and can be done in a day. What follows takes longer: examination, replies to objections and possible opposition. See the trademark guide.

  • Legal Metrology (packaged commodities) registration. For manufacturers, packers and importers of pre-packaged goods, along with the mandatory label declarations.

  • Trade licence. A municipal licence for specified trades and premises, such as eating places, clinics and workshops.

  • Provident fund and ESI. Employer registrations that become compulsory once headcount crosses the statutory limits. See the Labour Codes guide.

  • Professional tax. Levied by some states. Haryana and Delhi do not levy it.

  • Digital signature certificate. Needed by directors and authorised signatories for company, trademark and several other filings.

Licences that take more work

Some approvals need inspections, technical documents or a regulator's scrutiny, and should be planned for early: FSSAI State and Central licences, drug and cosmetic licences, BIS certification under quality-control orders, pollution control consents, factory licences, producer-responsibility registrations for plastic packaging and e-waste, and financial-sector registrations such as an NBFC certificate from the Reserve Bank. See NBFC and fintech.

What the work involves

  • Mapping. A written list of the registrations the business needs now, the ones it will need at the next stage, and the ones it does not need at all.

  • Applications. Preparing and filing the application in the correct name, category and class, and answering queries raised by the department.

  • Structure first. Deciding whether the business should run as a proprietorship, partnership, LLP or company before registrations are taken, since most of them attach to the entity and cannot simply be moved.

  • After registration. A calendar of returns, renewals and display requirements, and amendments when the address, activity or ownership changes.

  • Notices. Replies to show-cause notices, inspection reports and penalty orders from licensing authorities. See replying to a legal notice.

  • Businesses entering India. Registrations for foreign companies and their Indian entities, importers and distributors. See India legal counsel for foreign companies.

Questions founders ask

Which registration is the easiest to get for a small business in India? Udyam registration. It is free, fully online, needs no documents to be uploaded and is issued on the basis of Aadhaar and PAN, usually the same day.

Does every business need GST registration? No. It is compulsory only above the turnover threshold or in specified cases, such as supplying goods to another state or selling goods through an online marketplace. Many small local service businesses are below the threshold, though some register voluntarily because their customers want tax invoices.

Does a home-based food business need an FSSAI registration? Yes. Anyone who makes or sells food commercially must hold an FSSAI registration or licence, whatever the scale. The turnover decides only which category applies.

Can one person hold all the registrations in their own name? A sole proprietor can, since the proprietor and the business are the same person in law. If the business may later become a company or take investment, it is usually better to decide the structure first, because GST, FSSAI, IEC and trademarks then have to be transferred or taken afresh.

How long do these registrations take? Udyam and IEC are usually issued within a day or two. GST and shops and establishments registrations typically take a few working days if the documents are in order. FSSAI basic registration takes longer where the authority raises a query, and licences that need inspection can take several weeks.
