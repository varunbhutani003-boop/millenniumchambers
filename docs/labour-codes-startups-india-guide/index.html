India's Labour Codes: A Startup Guide

By Varun Bhutani, Advocate · Millennium Chambers, Gurugram · Updated 12 November 2026

What changed on 21 November 2025

The Government of India brought all four Labour Codes into force on 21 November 2025: the Code on Wages, 2019; the Code on Social Security, 2020; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. Together they consolidate 29 earlier central labour laws. Several operational details still depend on rules framed by the Centre and the States, so startups should track notifications for the States in which they operate.

Key points for Indian startups

Appointment letters for every worker

Employers must issue written appointment letters to all workers. Founders hiring their first employees, interns or contract staff should keep a standard appointment letter template ready.

Fixed-term employees and gratuity

Fixed-term employees become eligible for gratuity after one year of service, instead of five. Startups that hire on fixed-term contracts should review offer letters and payroll provisions.

Gig and platform workers

"Gig work", "platform work" and "aggregators" are defined in law for the first time. Aggregators are required to contribute 1–2% of annual turnover towards social security for gig and platform workers, capped at 5% of the amount paid or payable to such workers.

Wages and timely payment

Minimum wages extend to all workers, with a statutory floor wage. For the IT and ITES sector, salaries must be released by the 7th of every month.

Health, safety and working conditions

Employers must provide a free annual health check-up to workers above 40 years of age. Women may work night shifts with their consent and subject to required safety measures. ESIC coverage is voluntary for establishments with fewer than 10 employees, and mandatory even for one employee engaged in hazardous processes.

A short compliance checklist

  • Review appointment letter, offer letter and HR policy templates

  • Recheck salary structures and payroll against the new definition of wages

  • Map fixed-term, contract, intern and gig engagements and their entitlements

  • Update registrations, returns and digital filings as State rules are notified

  • Revisit standing orders, grievance and safety policies as headcount grows
