FSSAI Registration and Licence: The 2026 Turnover Limits

By Varun Bhutani, Advocate · Millennium Chambers, Gurugram · Updated 20 November 2026

What changed

Every food business in India needs an FSSAI registration or licence under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006. Which of the two it needs depends mainly on annual turnover, and those turnover bands were revised in 2026 for the first time in more than a decade.

The Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Amendment Regulations, 2026 were notified in the Gazette on 10 March 2026. An FSSAI order dated 13 March 2026 then fixed new turnover thresholds with effect from 1 April 2026.

The new categories

  • Registration: annual turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore. The earlier ceiling was Rs 12 lakh.

  • State Licence: turnover above Rs 1.5 crore and up to Rs 50 crore. The earlier band ran from Rs 12 lakh to Rs 20 crore.

  • Central Licence: turnover above Rs 50 crore. The earlier trigger was Rs 20 crore.

The government fee for basic registration is Rs 100 a year. Applications are made online on the FoSCoS portal.

What the change does not do

The Rs 1.5 crore figure decides the category. It is not an exemption. A food business below it still needs a registration before it starts selling, and one that operates without any registration or licence remains exposed to penalty under the Act.

Turnover is also not the only test. The portal's Kind of Business matrix places some activities in a higher category whatever the turnover. Importers of food, for example, need a Central Licence. A business should identify its activity first and its turnover second.

Other changes in the 2026 amendments

  • Validity. Registrations and licences are now granted with perpetual validity in place of fixed terms needing periodic renewal, subject to the conditions in the regulations. The details of what must still be paid or filed each year should be checked on the portal for the category concerned.

  • Street food vendors. The amendments address the double requirement that applied to vendors already registered under the street vending law.

  • Pending applications. FSSAI has clarified that applications under process on 1 April 2026 and granted afterwards are issued under the new framework.

What a small food business should do

  • Check the category again. A cloud kitchen, home baker, packaged-snack brand or cafe with turnover under Rs 1.5 crore that earlier needed a State Licence may now fall under registration for new applications.

  • Existing licence holders should read FSSAI's guidance on migration before letting anything lapse or applying afresh.

  • Match the activity. The registration should list every activity actually carried on: manufacturing, repacking, storage, retail, online sale. Marketplaces and food-delivery platforms check this before listing a seller.

  • Keep the label in step. The FSSAI number and logo go on the pack, and labelling and claims rules apply at every size of business.

  • Modify when things change. A new address, product category or change in ownership needs a modification of the registration or licence.

Why it matters beyond compliance

Marketplaces, modern retail chains and institutional buyers ask for the FSSAI number before onboarding a food brand, and investors check the category and the activities listed during due diligence. A registration in the wrong name or category is an easy fix early and an awkward one in the middle of a deal.
