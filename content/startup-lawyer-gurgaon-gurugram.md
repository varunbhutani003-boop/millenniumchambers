### How the work is arranged

Most startups do not need a full-time lawyer, and many cannot justify one before Series A. Work is taken either matter by matter, for example a funding round, a contract set or a single dispute, or on a monthly retainer where the practice acts as the company's outside legal team and handles whatever comes up. ### Questions founders ask

**When should a startup first speak to a lawyer?**
Before the co-founders split equity and before the first outside money or the first large customer contract. Fixing a founders' agreement, IP ownership or a cap table after a dispute or during due diligence costs far more than getting it right at the start.

**Should a startup hire an in-house lawyer or use an outside lawyer?**
Up to roughly Series A, most startups are better served by an outside lawyer on a retainer or per-matter basis. A full-time hire makes sense when contract volume, regulatory work or litigation becomes a daily load.
