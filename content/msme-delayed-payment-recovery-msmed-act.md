# MSME Payment Recovery Under the MSMED Act: A Guide for Startups

## Delayed payments to micro and small enterprises: how the MSMED Act works

Many Indian startups sit on both sides of the MSME payment problem. A young SaaS company, D2C brand or logistics startup registered as a micro or small enterprise often waits months for large customers to pay. The same startup, once it grows, buys from MSME vendors and must pay them on time. Chapter V of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 (the MSMED Act) governs both situations. This guide explains the rules in plain terms. It is general information, not legal advice.

### Who can use the MSMED Act payment protections

The delayed-payment protections in sections 15 to 24 apply to a "supplier", which the Act defines as a **micro or small** enterprise that has filed its memorandum (today, an Udyam registration). Medium enterprises are classified under the Act but are not "suppliers" for these provisions. The buyer can be any business, including a large company, a government body or another startup.

### The 45-day rule (section 15)

A buyer must pay a micro or small supplier on or before the date agreed in writing. The agreed period cannot exceed **45 days** from the day the goods or services are accepted (or deemed accepted). If there is no written agreement on payment terms, payment is due within 15 days of acceptance. A contract clause allowing 60 or 90 days does not override this limit.

### Interest on late payment (sections 16 and 17)

If the buyer pays late, it owes **compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India**, from the date payment fell due. This applies notwithstanding any contrary agreement. Under section 17, the buyer is liable to pay the principal together with this interest.

### Recovery through the MSE Facilitation Council (section 18)

A supplier (or either party to the dispute) can refer the dispute to the **Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC)** of the state where the supplier is located. Applications can be filed online on the Government of India's MSME Samadhaan portal. The process works in two stages:
- **Conciliation:** the Council first tries to settle the dispute, either itself or through an institution, using the conciliation provisions of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.
- **Arbitration:** if conciliation fails, the Council takes up the dispute for arbitration or refers it to an arbitral institution. The Act expects the reference to be decided within 90 days.

In *Gujarat State Civil Supplies Corporation v. Mahakali Foods* (2022), the Supreme Court held that Chapter V has overriding effect, so a supplier can approach the MSEFC even where the contract contains a private arbitration clause.

### Challenging an award: the 75% deposit (section 19)

A buyer that wants to set aside an MSEFC award or decree must first deposit **75% of the amount awarded**. The court may release part of this deposit to the supplier while the challenge is pending. This makes delaying tactics expensive for buyers.

### Registration timing: an open question

In *Silpi Industries v. Kerala SRTC* (2021) the Supreme Court said a supplier must be registered before the contract to claim the Act's benefits. In *NBCC (India) Ltd. v. State of West Bengal* (January 2025), a two-judge bench observed that registration is not a precondition for invoking section 18 and referred the question to a larger bench. Until that is settled, startups should obtain Udyam registration early and keep it current.

### If your startup is the buyer

- **Income tax:** under section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, introduced by the Finance Act 2023, payments due to micro and small enterprises are deductible only when actually paid if they are paid later than the MSMED Act time limit. For tax years from April 2026, check the corresponding provision of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- **Company disclosures:** companies must disclose amounts and interest due to micro and small enterprises in their financial statements (section 22), and specified companies file a half-yearly MSME Form I with the MCA when payments remain outstanding beyond 45 days.
- **Vendor onboarding:** ask vendors for their Udyam status at onboarding and align purchase-order payment terms to 45 days.

### Practical checklist for a supplier startup

- Keep your Udyam registration and classification current.
- Put payment terms (45 days or less) in the purchase order or contract.
- Record acceptance of goods or services, for example sign-offs or delivery emails.
- Raise invoices promptly and track due dates.
- Send a written reminder or legal notice citing sections 15 and 16 before filing.
- File on MSME Samadhaan with invoices, purchase orders and correspondence.
