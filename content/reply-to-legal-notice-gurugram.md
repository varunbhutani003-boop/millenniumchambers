# How to Reply to a Legal Notice: A Guide for Businesses in Gurugram

## Received a legal notice? What it is and what happens next

A legal notice is a formal letter, usually sent by an advocate on behalf of a client, that sets out a claim and asks the recipient to do something by a deadline: pay an amount, perform a contract, stop an activity or withdraw a statement. For startups and small businesses in Gurugram (Gurgaon), the most common notices concern unpaid invoices, dishonoured cheques, employee and vendor disputes, consumer complaints, trademark use and lease defaults. This guide explains how a reply works and the points to check before sending one. It is general information only, not legal advice on any particular notice.

### Is a legal notice the same as a case?

No. A notice is not a court order and, by itself, it does not create a liability. It is a step that usually comes before proceedings. In some situations the law makes a notice compulsory before a case can be filed, which is why the timelines in the notice matter:
- **Cheque dishonour (Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881):** the payee must send a written demand within 30 days of learning that the cheque was returned unpaid. The drawer then has **15 days from receiving the notice** to pay. If payment is not made, a complaint can be filed within one month after those 15 days end.
- **Suits against the Government (Section 80, Code of Civil Procedure):** two months' prior notice is generally required before suing a government or a public officer.
- **Commercial disputes (Section 12A, Commercial Courts Act, 2015):** a commercial suit that does not seek urgent interim relief must first go through pre-institution mediation, which is normally to be completed within three months (extendable by two months). The Supreme Court held this step mandatory in *Patil Automation v. Rakheja Engineers* (2022).
- **MSME supplier dues:** a micro or small supplier may instead approach the MSE Facilitation Council under the MSMED Act. See our guide to [MSME payment recovery under the MSMED Act](/msme-delayed-payment-recovery-msmed-act).

### Do you have to reply?

There is generally no legal duty to reply to a notice, and no fixed statutory time for a reply outside cases like Section 138. The deadline written in the notice (often 7, 15 or 30 days) is the sender's demand, not a rule of law. Even so, a considered reply is usually sensible. It puts your version of the facts on record early, it can correct a wrong amount or a wrong party, and it may open a route to settlement. Silence is sometimes presented later as if the claim was not disputed.

### First steps when a notice arrives

1. **Note the date of receipt** and keep the envelope, courier slip or email header. Several timelines run from the date of receipt.
2. **Identify the claim and its legal basis**: contract, cheque, consumer law, employment, intellectual property or something else.
3. **Collect the documents**: the contract or purchase order, invoices, emails and WhatsApp messages, delivery records, ledgers and any earlier correspondence.
4. **Check the numbers**: principal, interest rate, the period claimed and any payments already made or credit notes issued.
5. **Check who signed and who is named**: whether the notice is addressed to the company, a director personally or the wrong entity.
6. **Look for a contract clause** on notices, jurisdiction, arbitration or limitation of liability.

### What a reply usually contains

A reply is normally sent on the advocate's letterhead and covers:
- a reference to the notice, its date and the date of receipt;
- the facts in your own words, in date order;
- a point-by-point response to each allegation, saying clearly which points are denied;
- the legal position, including any contract terms, set-off, counter-claims or defects in the notice;
- what you are willing to do (if anything), and on what terms; and
- a reservation of all your rights and remedies.

### Points that need particular care

- **Admissions:** a written and signed acknowledgment of a debt, made before the limitation period expires, starts a fresh period of limitation under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963. Words in a reply can therefore extend the time the other side has to sue.
- **Without-prejudice offers:** settlement proposals are usually made in a separate communication marked "without prejudice", so they are not read as an admission.
- **Tone:** the reply may be read later by a judge, arbitrator or consumer commission. Factual and measured replies age better than angry ones.
- **Consistency:** what you say in the reply should match your documents and anything you may later state in a written statement or defence.
- **Delivery and proof:** send the reply by a method that gives proof, such as speed post or registered post with tracking, and by email where the notice came by email. Keep copies.

### Where disputes from Gurugram are usually heard

Depending on the claim and the contract, a dispute may go to the civil courts or commercial courts at the District Courts in Gurugram, the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission in Gurugram, the Haryana MSE Facilitation Council, an arbitral tribunal named in the contract, or the National Company Law Tribunal under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. Jurisdiction clauses in contracts and the place where the cause of action arose decide which forum applies.

### Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have to reply to a legal notice in India?**
There is no general statutory period for replying. The notice usually states a deadline, and in a cheque-bounce matter under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act the drawer has 15 days from receiving the notice to make payment.

**Can I ignore a legal notice?**
You can, but it is rarely wise. Ignoring it does not stop the sender from going to court, and a timely reply puts your position on record.

**Should my reply admit part of the amount?**
Any admission should be considered carefully, because a signed written acknowledgment of a debt can extend the limitation period under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963.

**Who should send the reply?**
Replies are commonly sent through an advocate, but a party may also reply on its own behalf. Either way, the reply should be accurate, consistent with the documents and sent with proof of delivery.
