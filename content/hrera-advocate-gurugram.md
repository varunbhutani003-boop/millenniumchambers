## RERA matters before the Haryana Real Estate Regulatory Authority, Gurugram

### When a RERA complaint is usually considered

- **Delayed possession.** The builder has missed the possession date in the agreement for sale, and the buyer wants either a refund with interest or interest for every month of delay while staying in the project.

- **Refund after cancellation.** The project is stalled, the registration has lapsed, or the builder cannot deliver, and the buyer wants the money back.

- **Changes to the plan or specifications.** The layout, area or amenities differ from what was sanctioned and promised.

- **Defects after possession.** Structural defects or poor workmanship reported within five years of handing over possession.

- **Unfair demands.** Charges or escalations not supported by the agreement, or a forfeiture larger than the law allows.

### The basic legal framework

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (the RERA Act) applies to registered projects. The provisions most often used in Gurugram complaints are:

- **Section 18:** if the promoter fails to give possession as per the agreement, the allottee can withdraw and claim a refund with interest, or stay in the project and claim interest for every month of delay until possession.

- **Section 19:** the allottee's rights to information about the project, to possession, and to the documents and common areas.

- **Section 31:** any aggrieved person can file a complaint with the Authority (or the adjudicating officer) for a violation of the Act, rules or regulations.

- **Section 71:** compensation claims are decided by the adjudicating officer, separately from refund and interest relief before the Authority.

- **Section 40:** amounts ordered and not paid can be recovered as arrears of land revenue.

- **Sections 43 and 44:** an appeal against an order lies to the Haryana Real Estate Appellate Tribunal within 60 days. A promoter who appeals must first deposit at least 30% of the penalty, or the full amount payable to the allottee with interest, as the case may be.

Interest rates under the Haryana rules are linked to the State Bank of India's marginal cost of lending rate, so the figure is worked out for each case.

### How a complaint generally proceeds

1. **Documents first.** Agreement for sale or builder-buyer agreement, allotment letter, payment receipts and the bank statement trail, demand letters, the project's RERA registration and any extension, and correspondence with the builder.

2. **Checking the forum and the relief.** Refund and delay interest go to the Authority; compensation goes to the adjudicating officer. Asking for the wrong relief in the wrong form is a common cause of delay.

3. **Filing on the Haryana RERA portal**, with the prescribed form, fee and documents, and serving the builder.

4. **Hearings**, where the builder files a reply and both sides are heard. Many matters settle once a hearing date is set.

5. **Order and enforcement.** If the builder does not pay within the time given, execution proceedings follow.

### Commercial and office space

RERA is not only for flats. Companies and founders who have booked office or retail space in registered commercial projects in Gurugram have the same rights on possession and refunds. For leases of ready office space, the issues are contractual instead: lock-in periods, security deposits, escalation and early exit. ### Common questions

**Which RERA authority hears complaints about projects in Gurugram?**
The Haryana Real Estate Regulatory Authority, Gurugram hears complaints about projects in Gurugram district. Projects elsewhere in Haryana go to the Authority at Panchkula.

**Can I get both a refund and compensation?**
Refund with interest, or delay interest, is decided by the Authority under Section 18. Compensation is a separate claim before the adjudicating officer under Section 71, and is assessed on the facts.

**How long do I have to appeal an HRERA order?**
An appeal lies to the Haryana Real Estate Appellate Tribunal within 60 days of receiving the order. The Tribunal can allow a later appeal if there was sufficient cause for the delay.
