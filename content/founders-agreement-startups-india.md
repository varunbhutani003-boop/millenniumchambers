# Founders' Agreement for Startups in India

## Founders' agreements in India: what co-founders should put in writing

A founders' agreement records how co-founders will own, run and, if needed, part ways with their startup. Many Indian startups sign one before or soon after incorporation and later carry its key terms into a shareholders' agreement and the company's Articles of Association. This note by Advocate Varun Bhutani of Millennium Chambers, Gurugram, summarises the points founders commonly cover. It is general information only, not legal advice.

### 1. Equity split and vesting

The agreement should state how many shares each founder holds and whether those shares vest over time. A common market practice is vesting over about four years with a one-year cliff. Because Indian companies issue shares upfront, "vesting" is usually built as reverse vesting: if a founder leaves early, the company or the other founders can buy back the unvested shares at an agreed price, under transfer provisions in the shareholders' agreement and the Articles.

### 2. Roles, time commitment and decisions

Record each founder's role and title, whether they will work full-time, and which decisions need the consent of all founders, for example raising funds, issuing new shares, taking on large debt or hiring senior staff. A simple deadlock mechanism, such as escalation to an adviser or mediation, avoids paralysis later.

### 3. Intellectual property

Code, brand names, designs and content created for the business should belong to the company. Under Section 19 of the Copyright Act, 1957, an assignment of copyright must be in writing and signed by the assignor, and should identify the work, the rights assigned, the duration and the territory. If the duration is not stated it is deemed to be five years, and if the territory is not stated it is presumed to be India. Each founder, and any early freelancer, should therefore sign a clear written IP assignment in favour of the company. Trademarks should be filed in the company's name (see our[trademark registration guide for startups](/trademark-registration-startups-india)).

### 4. Confidentiality, non-compete and non-solicitation

Confidentiality obligations can continue after a founder leaves. Non-compete clauses need more care: Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 makes an agreement that restrains anyone from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business void, subject to a limited exception for the sale of goodwill. Indian courts have generally upheld restrictions that apply while a person is still engaged with the business, but not broad restraints after the relationship ends (see the Supreme Court's decisions in Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning (1967) and Percept D'Mark v. Zaheer Khan (2006)). Founders should draft post-exit restrictions with this in mind.

### 5. Leaving the company

Set out what happens if a founder resigns, is removed, or can no longer work because of death or incapacity: "good leaver" and "bad leaver" terms, how the price of a leaver's shares will be worked out, and rights of first refusal, tag-along and drag-along on future share transfers.

### 6. Disputes

A clause providing for negotiation, then mediation, then arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, with a stated seat and governing law, keeps disagreements private and quicker to resolve.

## Making the terms binding on the company

A private contract between founders does not automatically bind the company. The Supreme Court held in V.B. Rangaraj v. V.B. Gopalakrishnan (1992) that restrictions on share transfer which are not in the Articles of Association do not bind the company or its shareholders. When the company is incorporated, or when it raises its first round, founders usually sign a shareholders' agreement and amend the Articles so the key terms match.

Stamp duty is a State subject in India and rates vary from State to State. Under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, an instrument that is not duly stamped cannot be admitted in evidence until the duty and any penalty are paid, so founders should stamp the agreement correctly when it is signed.

## Founders' agreement checklist

- Shareholding of each founder agreed and recorded

- Vesting or reverse vesting, with a cliff and a buy-back price

- Written IP assignment signed by every founder and early contributor

- Roles, time commitment and decisions needing unanimous consent

- Deadlock and leaver terms, including how shares will be valued

- Confidentiality and realistic non-solicitation and non-compete terms

- Dispute resolution clause with seat and governing law

- Agreement duly stamped; key terms carried into the Articles of Association
