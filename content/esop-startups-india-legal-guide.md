# ESOPs for Startups in India

## ESOPs for Indian startups: the legal basics

An Employee Stock Option Plan (ESOP) gives employees the right, but not the obligation, to buy shares of the company at a pre-set price after the options vest. For early-stage Indian startups, ESOPs are a common way to attract and keep talent while conserving cash. This note summarises the framework for private (unlisted) companies.

### The legal framework

For unlisted companies, ESOPs are issued under Section 62(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013 read with Rule 12 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014. Listed companies are governed separately by SEBI's share-based employee benefits regulations.

### Key requirements under Rule 12

- Shareholder approval: the ESOP scheme must be approved by a special resolution of the shareholders.

- Minimum vesting period: there must be at least one year between the grant of options and their vesting.

- Who can receive options: permanent employees and directors (other than independent directors). Promoters, and directors who hold more than 10% of the equity, are generally excluded; a DPIIT-recognised startup may grant options to them for up to ten years from its incorporation.

- No transfer: options are personal to the employee and cannot be transferred, pledged or mortgaged.

- Records: the company must maintain a register of employee stock options in Form SH-6 and make the prescribed disclosures in its Board's report.

### Tax at a glance

In general, the difference between the fair market value of the shares on the date of exercise and the exercise price is taxed in the employee's hands as a salary perquisite, and any later gain on sale of the shares is taxed as capital gains. Eligible startups have a special regime that defers tax on the perquisite. Tax rules change and depend on facts, so specific tax advice should be taken.

### Founder checklist

- Size the option pool and check it against your shareholders' agreement and investor consent rights.

- Adopt a written ESOP scheme covering eligibility, vesting, exercise price and period, and what happens on exit or termination.

- Pass the board resolution and the shareholders' special resolution, and file the resolution with the Registrar where required.

- Issue clear grant letters and keep the SH-6 register up to date.

- Explain the tax treatment to employees before they exercise.
