### Regulatory Watch

- SEBI: online KYC from abroad for NRIs, OCIs and foreign nationals — effective 3 December 2026. Circular HO/38/30/12(3)2026-MIRSD-POD2/I/25175/2026 (4 Nov 2026) lets SEBI-registered intermediaries such as brokers and depository participants complete digital KYC for individual persons resident outside India who live in FATF-compliant countries, without the client being physically present in India. Video in-person verification needs geo-tagging, liveness and IP checks, PAN is verified against the Income Tax database, a passport copy (and OCI card, if any) is required, and KYC records must reach the KRA within 3 working days. Clients in FATF non-compliant countries stay under the existing process.

- CERT-In advisory on AI-driven attacks on payment and disbursement APIs — issued 4 November 2026 (advisory; applies immediately as guidance). Advisory CIAD-2026-0058 warns banks, NBFCs, fintechs, payment aggregators and crypto exchanges of a threat actor targeting payment and disbursement APIs and non-production environments. It recommends least-privilege access for service and AI agents, separating test sandboxes from production secrets, immutable audit logs, throttling disbursement APIs, validating callbacks and reconciling payouts hourly. Reports indicate affected entities should also report through RBI's DAKSH portal, on top of existing CERT-In incident-reporting duties.

### Founder Takeaway

Private companies other than small companies must issue and transfer securities only in demat form (Rule 9B, Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014), so NRI or OCI co-founders, angels and ESOP holders usually need an Indian demat account. Startups with such holders in FATF-compliant countries can plan pending allotments, ESOP exercises and share transfers around the 3 December 2026 start date, and check that each holder's PAN, passport and overseas address details are current; FEMA reporting for share issues and transfers to non-residents continues to apply.

### Regulatory Watch

- FEMA: lighter export-reporting for service exporters — effective 1 October 2026. RBI Notification No. FEMA 23(R)/(2)/2026-RB dated 2 November 2026 amends the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 with retrospective effect from 1 October 2026. Companies, LLPs and other non-individual service exporters are deemed to have filed the Export Declaration Form (EDF) where the inward remittance is up to ₹10 lakh and the bank has the payment information it needs; resident individuals get deemed filing irrespective of amount. AD banks now record service imports in IDPMS only where the importer is not a resident individual and the invoice or contract value exceeds ₹10 lakh. An earlier amendment (FEMA 23(R)/(1)/2026-RB, 22 September 2026, also effective 1 October 2026) sets the export realisation period at nine months (twelve months for exports invoiced or settled in rupees).

- RBI Master Directions on cyber resilience for non-bank payment operators — dated 5 November 2026. The Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank PSOs (RBI/DPSS/2026-27/475) apply immediately (from placement on the RBI website) to large and medium operators such as payment aggregators, card networks, TReDS platforms, large and medium PPI issuers and inbound money-transfer operators, and from 1 April 2028 to small PPI issuers and instant money-transfer operators. They require board-level oversight of cyber risk, reporting of cyber attacks, critical outages, internal fraud and settlement delays to RBI within six hours of detection (cyber incidents also to CERT-In), multi-factor authentication for electronic debits and an annual independent system audit; several older system-audit and ATM-switch circulars are repealed.

### Founder Takeaway

Startups billing overseas clients for software or services can check, invoice by invoice, which receipts fall within the ₹10 lakh deemed-EDF route and keep the information their bank asks for on file; larger receipts still follow the regular EDF process. With realisation now at nine months, it is worth reviewing payment terms in foreign customer contracts and tracking overdue receivables. Payment-sector startups can map which PSO category they fall in and test whether their incident-response process can meet the six-hour reporting window.

### Regulatory Watch

- GST e-invoicing extended to reverse-charge self-invoices and imports of services — effective 1 January 2027. Notification No. 04/2026–Central Tax (26 Oct 2026), issued under Rule 48(4) of the CGST Rules, amends Notification 13/2020. Businesses already under e-invoicing (aggregate turnover above ₹5 Cr in any financial year since 2017-18) will need an IRN for self-invoices on supplies received from unregistered persons under reverse charge and on imports of services, such as foreign cloud, software and advertising services. The e-invoice schema (v1.2) adds supply-type codes RCMDOM and RCMIMP; existing exclusions (SEZ units, banks, NBFCs, insurers, GTAs, among others) are unchanged.

- SEBI sets up a Working Group to review the Innovators Growth Platform (IGP) — constituted 4 Nov 2026 (Press Release No. 69/2026); no rule changes in force yet. Co-chaired by Srini Srinivasan (IVCA) and Prashanth Prakash (Accel), the 18-member group will review IGP eligibility (including research-stage, pre-revenue and IP-led companies), investor participation, issue pricing and allocation, post-listing obligations and migration to the main board, benchmarked against overseas frameworks. Its recommendations go to SEBI for consideration; no deadline has been announced.

### Founder Takeaway

Companies above the ₹5 Cr e-invoicing threshold can use the next seven weeks to list recurring foreign-vendor payments (cloud, SaaS, ad platforms) and reverse-charge purchases from unregistered suppliers, and to confirm that their accounting or billing software can raise these self-invoices with the RCMIMP/RCMDOM codes and obtain IRNs before 1 January 2027. Deep-tech founders weighing an Indian listing may track the IGP working group's recommendations, as eligibility norms for pre-revenue companies are under review.

Remote-access, device and data-security arrangements for staff working from home are also worth reviewing for the period.

### Regulatory Watch

1. SEBI revises the Accredited Investor framework (Circular No. HO/19/34/11(6)2026-AFD-POD1/I/25244/2026, dated 5 November 2026; reported to apply with immediate effect). Managers of AIFs, SIFs and portfolio management services may now determine and record an investor's accredited status themselves, at onboarding or during a scheme's tenure, as an alternative to an accreditation agency certificate. Accreditation is valid for three years and is recognised by other managers in the same group. An LLP qualifies only if each partner meets the criteria, and persons resident outside India are treated as deemed accredited investors (AML and due-diligence checks still apply). This matters for angel funds: under SEBI's 7 September 2026 circular, angel funds registered on or before 10 September 2025 must move to an accredited-investors-only model by 31 March 2027.

2. GST: new Rule 14B registration for e-commerce sellers, in force from 1 November 2026 (CBIC Notification No. 05/2026–Central Tax, dated 26 October 2026). A seller already registered in one State can register in another State where it has no premises by declaring a section 52 e-commerce operator's warehouse as its principal place of business. The registration can be used only for supplies through that operator; output tax on supplies to registered persons is capped at ₹2.5 lakh a month; the operator must consent within seven working days; and only one such registration is allowed per State per PAN. If verification is not completed within 30 days of the ARN, the application is deemed approved. GSTN enabled the option on the portal from 1 November 2026.

### Founder Takeaway

Founders planning to raise from angel funds in the coming months may wish to confirm with the fund how its investors are being accredited, since older angel funds cannot accept contributions from non-accredited investors after 31 March 2027, which could affect the timing of rounds closing in early 2027. D2C brands selling through marketplaces may review whether Rule 14B lets them stock inventory in other-State marketplace warehouses without separate premises, keeping in mind the ₹2.5 lakh monthly cap on B2B output tax.

### Regulatory Watch

1. DPDP: Consent Manager registration opens on 13 November 2026. Rule 4 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), published 13 November 2025) comes into force one year after publication, i.e. on 13 November 2026. From that date, companies can apply to the Data Protection Board to register as Consent Managers; the First Schedule requires, among other conditions, a company incorporated in India with a net worth of at least ₹2 crore. The core duties for businesses (notice and consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, children's data, data principal rights — Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23) apply from 13 May 2027. A January 2026 proposal to shorten that 18-month window has not been notified so far.

2. RBI shifts FEMA compounding for overseas investment, borrowing/lending and guarantee contraventions to Regional Offices (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 28/2026-27, dated 23 October 2026; no later commencement date is stated). Overseas investment cases go to the Regional Office matching the UIN prefix of the foreign entity (for example, New Delhi for ND, CG, JM, JR, KA, PT and WR); borrowing/lending and guarantee cases go to the Regional Office with jurisdiction over the applicant's registered office. Cases without a UIN, or covering several categories, stay with CEFA at the Central Office, Mumbai.

### Founder Takeaway

The 13 November 2026 date directly affects only companies planning to operate as Consent Managers. For most startups the operative DPDP date is 13 May 2027, about six months away. Typical preparatory steps include mapping what personal data is collected and where it flows, revising privacy notices and consent journeys, reviewing vendor contracts for data-processing terms, and assigning responsibility for breach reporting.

### Regulatory Watch

1. MCA extends FY 2025-26 annual filing deadlines (General Circular No. 05/2026, dated 24 Oct 2026). Companies may file financial statements in e-form AOC-4 (including the CFS, XBRL and NBFC Ind AS variants) up to 31 December 2026, and annual returns in MGT-7/MGT-7A up to 31 January 2027, without additional fees. The circular states that it does not extend the statutory time for holding AGMs, and that filings after these dates attract all fees, including additional fees, under the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014. General Circular No. 06/2026 (27 Oct 2026) gives similar relief for CRA-4 cost audit reports up to 31 December 2026.

2. MSMED (Amendment) Act, 2026 — first provisions in force from 15 October 2026 (Ministry of MSME notification S.O. 5575(E), dated 12 Oct 2026). Now in force: classification of enterprises by investment in plant, machinery or equipment alongside turnover; free, voluntary registration on a notified national digital platform; a new Section 15A on settling MSME invoices of central public sector enterprises through RBI-authorised TReDS platforms; disclosure of TReDS-routed invoices by specified entities; and a revised penalty and adjudication framework. Section 12 (composition of MSE Facilitation Councils) takes effect on 15 December 2026; Sections 8–10 (dispute resolution changes) have no commencement date yet.

### Founder Takeaway

The MCA relief is about filing fees, not the AGM: the AGM timeline (for most companies, by 30 September 2026 for FY 2025-26) is unchanged. The extended windows close on 31 December 2026 (AOC-4) and 31 January 2027 (MGT-7/7A), after which additional fees apply. Startups registered as MSMEs, or buying from them, may also want to track the rules to be prescribed under the amended MSMED Act.
