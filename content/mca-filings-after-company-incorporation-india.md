# MCA Filings After Incorporating a Company in India

## What a new private company has to file, and which forms it can file on its own

The certificate of incorporation is the start of a company's filing life, not the end of it. A handful of forms fall due in the first six months and another set repeats every year, whether or not the company has earned anything. Missing them is expensive: most late filings attract an additional fee that grows by the day, and a company that does not file for long enough can be struck off and its directors disqualified.

The good news for a small private company is that most of the routine forms are short, web-based and signed with a director's digital signature. This guide separates the forms a founder can realistically file on the MCA portal without outside help from the steps where the law requires a practising professional.

## The first 180 days

### 1. First board meeting and auditor

The board should meet within 30 days of incorporation. At or around that meeting the directors disclose their interests in other entities (Form MBP-1) and confirm that they are not disqualified (Form DIR-8). Both are kept in the company's records and are not filed with the Registrar. The board also appoints the first statutory auditor within 30 days, and the appointment is intimated to the Registrar in Form ADT-1.

### 2. Bring in the subscription money and issue share certificates

The subscribers pay for the shares they agreed to take in the memorandum, into the company's bank account. Share certificates are issued within 60 days of incorporation and stamped under the state's stamp law.

### 3. Declaration of commencement of business: Form INC-20A

A company with share capital files INC-20A within 180 days of incorporation, declaring that every subscriber has paid for their shares, with the bank statement as proof. The company cannot start business or borrow until it is filed. This form must be certified by a practising chartered accountant, company secretary or cost accountant.

### 4. Registered office and stationery

The company's name, registered office address, CIN and contact details go on a board outside the office and on letterheads and invoices. If incorporation was done with a correspondence address only, the registered office is notified in Form INC-22 within 30 days.

## Every year

- **Board meetings.** A small company holds at least two board meetings a year, one in each half, at least 90 days apart. Other private companies hold four.

- **Books and audit.** Every company keeps books of account and has them audited by a chartered accountant each year, even with no turnover.

- **Annual general meeting.** Held within six months of the financial year end, which is 30 September for most companies. The first AGM can be held within nine months of the close of the first financial year.

- **Form AOC-4:** the audited financial statements, within 30 days of the AGM.

- **Form MGT-7A:** the abridged annual return for small companies and one person companies, within 60 days of the AGM. Other companies file Form MGT-7.

- **Form ADT-1:** within 15 days of the AGM at which an auditor is appointed, usually once in five years.

- **Form DPT-3:** by 30 June, where the company has outstanding loans or other money received that is not a deposit, such as a loan from a director.

- **Form MSME-1:** half-yearly, where payments to micro or small enterprise suppliers have been outstanding for more than 45 days.

- **Director KYC.** Since 31 March 2026 every person holding a DIN files Form DIR-3 KYC Web once in three financial years, by 30 June, and updates a change of mobile number, email or address when it happens. The earlier rule was an annual filing by 30 September.

## What a small company can file without a practising professional

A "small company" is a private company within the paid-up capital and turnover limits in the Companies Act. The limits have been raised more than once, most recently in 2025, and most early-stage private companies fall within them. For such a company and for a one person company, the following are signed by a director with a digital signature and need no certificate from a practising professional:

- **ADT-1**, auditor appointment.

- **DIR-3 KYC Web**, completed by the director personally with OTPs to mobile and email.

- **MSME-1**, the half-yearly return on dues to micro and small suppliers.

- **DPT-3**, the return of loans and deposits, in the ordinary case where the company has taken no public deposits.

- **MGT-7A**, the abridged annual return.

- **AOC-4**, the financial statements, where the company is a small company or a one person company. The accounts attached to it must still have been audited.

These are filed on the MCA V3 portal through the company's or a director's own login. A founder who is comfortable with the portal and keeps the register of members, minutes and loan details in order can file them personally.

## Where the law requires a professional

- **The statutory audit.** Only a chartered accountant in practice can audit the accounts, and there is no exemption for small or dormant companies. This is the one cost that cannot be avoided.

- **INC-20A.** Certification by a practising professional is mandatory.

- **Event-based forms.** Filings for a change of directors, a shift of registered office, an allotment of shares or an increase in authorised capital are, as a rule, certified by a practising professional, with limited exceptions. The certification column of the form should be checked each time.

- **Companies that are not small.** AOC-4 needs professional certification, and larger companies need a company secretary's certificate on the annual return.

- **Valuation.** A share allotment at a premium needs a valuation report from a registered valuer, and for tax purposes often a merchant banker or chartered accountant.

## Why self-filing still goes wrong

- **Dates.** The clock runs from the AGM date, and a wrong AGM date in one form carries into the next.

- **Mismatched figures.** The loan shown in DPT-3, the dues in MSME-1 and the shareholding in MGT-7A must match the audited accounts. Mismatches invite notices years later, often during an investor's due diligence.

- **Missed nil filings.** A company with no business still files its annual forms.

- **Minutes and registers.** The forms only report what the board and shareholders decided. If the resolutions and registers do not exist, the filing has nothing to stand on.

- **No correction button.** Most filed forms cannot simply be revised, and an error may need a fresh filing or an application for condonation or compounding.

## A short checklist for the first year

- First board meeting held, MBP-1 and DIR-8 taken on record.

- First auditor appointed within 30 days.

- Subscription money received and share certificates issued and stamped within 60 days.

- INC-20A filed within 180 days.

- Statutory registers and minutes book opened.

- AGM date fixed, with AOC-4 and MGT-7A dates counted from it.

- 30 June marked for DPT-3 and, in the applicable year, director KYC.
